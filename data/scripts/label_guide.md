# Labelling guide (extractor)

For each sentence, label every one of the six RIASEC types:
- R Realistic: hands-on work with tools, machines, equipment, materials, plants or animals, outdoors, building, fixing.
- I Investigative: figuring out why, research, analysing, experiments, data, solving puzzles or problems, science, coding to understand or debug.
- A Artistic: creating, designing, drawing, writing, music, performing, expressing ideas, freedom over how something looks or feels.
- S Social: helping, teaching, caring for, counselling or supporting people; working closely with people to benefit them.
- E Enterprising: leading, persuading, selling, pitching, negotiating, managing, starting a business, making the call.
- C Conventional: organising, records, spreadsheets, schedules, procedures, checking details, following a clear process.

## Direction per type
- positive: the student currently likes it or leans towards it, including weak acceptance ("I don't mind X", "X is fine", "either is okay").
- negative: the student currently dislikes it or leans away from it.
- neutral: no stance on that type. This includes not mentioning it, pure uncertainty ("not sure about X", "it depends", "idk"), and things they liked only in the past with no present view.

## Strength (only for positive or negative types)
- strong: emphatic ("love", "hate", "can't stand", "the best part", "really really", "so draining", exclamation-level intensity).
- mild: a plain or hedged like or dislike ("like", "quite enjoy", "not my thing", "prefer", "kinda", "probably", "more interesting than"), and indirect evidence of interest without an explicit evaluation.
- dontmind: weak acceptance only ("don't mind", "it's okay", "fine with it", "can do", "either also can").

## Reason (one per sentence)
- familiarity: the stance rests on exposure rather than enjoyment, e.g. "I did the accounting track so that's what I know", "my family is in sales so I'll probably do that". If the student says they enjoy (or do not enjoy) the thing itself, use enjoyment.
- enjoyment: any stance about liking or disliking the work itself.
- neither: no stance on any type (all six neutral).

## Hard cases
- A narrow exception does not flip the whole type: "I like teaching friends, just not presenting to big groups" gives S positive (mild). If the exception is about a different type, label that type.
- Ambivalence: "I don't mind research, but wouldn't want to do it all day" gives I positive, dontmind.
- Hedged lean: "I think I prefer solving problems, but I'm still figuring things out" gives I positive, mild. Pure uncertainty with no lean is neutral.
- Change over time: label the CURRENT stance. "I used to enjoy organising events, but lately research is more interesting" gives I positive (mild) and E neutral. If they say they no longer like it ("I used to like X but now it bores me"), that type is negative.
- Experience vs preference: "I've done accounting modules but never really enjoyed them" gives C negative (mild), reason enjoyment (they talk about enjoyment).
- Singapore English and slang count: "sian" (bored, fed up) is negative; "shiok" is strong positive; "can lah" is weak acceptance (dontmind); "cannot tahan" is strong negative.
